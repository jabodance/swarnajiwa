About Swarnajiwa

Swarna means gold, jiwa means soul. Just as Swarnadwipa, the old name of Sumatra, means “island of gold”, Swarnajiwa means “golden soul”: a few minutes a day to breathe, listen and return to the gold within.

The body as a small universe

In Javanese and Balinese thought, the body is Bhuwana Alit, the small world, a mirror of Bhuwana Agung, the great one. The figure at the centre of the cosmos is you.

Four companions, one centre

Javanese philosophy speaks of Sedulur Papat Lima Pancer: four companions born with us, linked to water, earth, fire and air, around a fifth at the centre, the self. The five sounds follow this map. Earth, Water, Fire and Wind are the four. Akasha, the open sky, is the pancer: your own still centre.

Seven points, seven tones

Each point has a familiar name, a quality to rest on, and a tone of the seven-note pelog gamelan scale, from Ji at the base to Pi above the crown.

Sound, colour and direction

Long before apps, Balinese palm-leaf manuscripts on gamelan, such as the Lontar Prakempa, linked tones with colours and the directions of the world. Swarnajiwa is a modern echo of that idea.

Our own embat

No two gamelan are tuned alike; each set has its own embat, its own character. Swarnajiwa has one too. Its Ji sits at 136 Hz, close to the average Ji measured on Javanese kempul gongs in Yogyakarta. There are no "healing frequencies" here, just tones chosen to feel warm and calm.

Care notes

  • Swarnajiwa is a self-care companion, not a substitute for professional help or treatment.
  • If anything feels uncomfortable, stop, open your eyes and breathe normally.
  • Start with the volume low. Gentle is enough.
  • Skip breath holds if you feel dizzy, are pregnant, or have a heart or lung condition.
